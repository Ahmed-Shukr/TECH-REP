An examination system fails when it is designed as a set of forms. It works when it is designed as a state machine: definition → sitting → attendance → marking → publication.

## First-class records

At minimum the domain needs:

- **Exam** — what is being assessed
- **Sitting** — when and where that exam happens
- **Candidate** — who is registered for that sitting
- **Mark / result** — the scored outcome, with status

If marks live as free text on a spreadsheet attachment, you do not have a system. You have a filing cabinet with a login.

## Status is a product feature

A mark that can be edited by anyone at any time is not an academic record. Useful statuses:

- draft
- submitted by examiner
- reviewed
- published

Each transition should be visible. Odoo's chatter is not decoration here; it is the audit trail the institution will need when a result is challenged.

## Role separation

The person who schedules sittings, the person who invigilates, and the person who marks are different jobs. Record rules should follow that split. A shared "Exam Manager" group is convenient in development and sloppy in production.

## What Odoo gives you

Odoo is a good fit because the hard parts — users, groups, workflows, reports, PostgreSQL — already exist. The custom work is the domain model and the sitting-day operating procedure, not a new framework.

The Exam Management Platform in this portfolio is built on that premise.
